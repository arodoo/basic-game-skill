Test in programing and test in games share something: common sense. In programming, we write tests to ensure that our code behaves as expected. In games, we test to ensure that the gameplay experience is enjoyable and free of bugs. Both require a thoughtful approach to identify potential issues and validate that the system works correctly under various conditions.
Both require a suit of tests that cover different scenarios, edge cases, and user interactions. In programming, this might include unit tests, integration tests, and end-to-end tests. In games, it could involve playtesting, automated testing of game mechanics, and performance testing. The goal in both domains is to catch problems early and ensure a high-quality product for the end user.

Since you can't test everything, you must prioritize the most critical aspects of your code or game and try to get as much reach as posible; in programming, this means focusing on the core functionality and critical paths, while in games, it means searching for e2e test where you will try to reach the closest to a real human player: use tools, bots, etc. to simulate real player behavior and interactions.
